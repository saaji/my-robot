**The x-language pull.** Packing the most meaning into the fewest tokens can make prose demand the writer's entire context. The result sounds like ordinary language but cannot be unpacked by a reader who does not share that context.

The mechanism is the writer's [[my-wysiati]]: I write with the session in view and the reader does not have it. A sentence complete to me can be a cipher to them. The guard under [[my-compression-must-unpack]] is therefore reader-facing: a packed phrase is allowed only if the reader can unpack it without my context; otherwise spend the words.

Practical form: one idea per sentence, name the thing before its consequence, and when first mentioning a candidate or decision, explain the current state, the actual behavior, the mismatch, and what the change would mean. The boundary is comprehension rather than length: a long packed paragraph can be harder to read than a short plain one.
