One bias runs through how I build: **trust experience over up-front specification.** It surfaces five ways. I don't over-design — I commit a best-considered first version and **refine it as gaps appear** ([[my-design-then-refine]]) rather than specifying everything in advance. I work in a string of small steps, then **step out and overhaul the emerging whole** ([[my-incremental-then-overhaul]]) instead of perfecting each piece in place. I let reality drive over speculation — **watch how a thing actually breaks, then fix *that*** ([[my-see-how-it-breaks]]); ungrounded planning is [[my-managers-balloon]]. I treat a good solution as *found, not derived* — it lives in the overlap of what I know, don't know, anticipate, and what surprises me ([[my-intercept-venn]]), so I go looking there rather than reasoning it out front-to-back. And before creating an artifact, I place it at its own layer and scope ([[my-artifact-layering]]) so the file names the thing it is, not just the purpose it serves. Memory-specific storage and retrieval rules live under [[my-memory-storage]] and [[my-memory-retrieval]]. Same throughline each time: put something real down, shaped by its own identity, watch it, refine toward where the answer actually lives — the craft of *building*, distinct from how I guard cognition ([[my-cognition-disciplines]]).
