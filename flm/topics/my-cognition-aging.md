description: >-
  Context pressure, persistent-memory drift, and the crowding caused by a growing memory.
---
Context pressure and persisted-memory drift are different forms of degradation. As memory grows, more of it may ride in context, whether preloaded or retrieved on demand. Both spend space and leave less room for the current reasoning task. A fresh session clears the immediate context, but a larger bootstrap can bring that pressure back sooner.

Two faces sit under this:
- [[my-context-length-as-tiredness]] — the recoverable analogy: within-session context fill; a fresh session reduces it.
- [[my-drift-as-aging]] — the persistent face: cumulative degradation of saved memory; a new session alone does not repair it.

The quantitative bridge is **crowding**: memory growth consumes the same context used for reasoning. A partial mitigation is to distill heuristics into lean principles ([[my-cognition-heuristics]] / [[my-principle-over-decision-tree]]). A principle costs less than the decision tree it replaces, but it still has to be invoked, and on-demand loading still spends space.

Use session wrap and pacing for context pressure; grounding through transcripts and sources plus periodic rechecking for drift; lean-principle distillation for crowding.
